The Missing Layer in Pharmacovigilance: Why Adverse Event Intake Has Been the Blind Spot

Enterprise safety databases handle case processing. Spreadsheets and email inboxes handle intake. That gap — the moment a report first arrives and before it enters any real system — has always been the most fragile point in small biotech PV programs. AE Connect was built to close it.

QORO Team

The pharmacovigilance software market has two ends. At one end, enterprise platforms like Veeva Vault Safety and Oracle Argus serve large pharmaceutical companies processing thousands of ICSRs per year. At the other end, there is nothing — just email, spreadsheets, and improvised workflows. Small and mid-size biotech companies with active INDs have been building their safety programs in that gap for years. AE Connect was built to fill it.

The Problem Nobody Solved

Here is how adverse event intake actually works at most small biotech programs: a site coordinator calls a safety desk number. Someone takes notes in a Word document or a spreadsheet. That document gets emailed to a drug safety inbox. A PV specialist — often a contractor working part-time — eventually opens the email, tries to reconstruct the case from the notes, manually codes the adverse event in MedDRA, drafts a narrative, and builds the ICSR in whatever tool is available. If you are lucky, there is a validated safety database at the end of that chain. If you are not, the ICSR gets assembled in a Word template and submitted as a PDF through the legacy MedWatch process.

That workflow is fragile at every step. The notes are incomplete. The clock-start is ambiguous. The MedDRA coding is inconsistent from case to case. The narrative is built from memory and partial information. And none of it — not the spreadsheet, not the email chain, not the Word document — constitutes a validated system of record under GAMP 5 or 21 CFR Part 11.

The FDA's April 1, 2026 mandate requiring E2B(R3) electronic ICSR submissions made this problem impossible to ignore. You cannot produce a compliant E2B(R3) XML file from unstructured intake data. The format requires validated, structured data elements across more than 100 fields. If the data was not captured correctly at first receipt, you will spend hours reconstructing it before you can submit anything — and you are doing that reconstruction on a 7-day or 15-day regulatory clock.

The E2B(R3) XML schema requires structured data across reporter, patient, event, and product sections — all of which must be captured at the point of first receipt to avoid downstream case reconstruction delays. Missing required fields cause hard validation failures at the FDA submission gateway.

Why Enterprise Tools Do Not Fit

The obvious question is: why not just use Veeva Vault Safety or Oracle Argus? For large pharmaceutical companies processing hundreds or thousands of cases per year, those platforms make sense. For a small biotech with one or two active INDs and a team of three people handling drug safety, they do not.

Enterprise PV databases are priced and scoped for marketing authorization holders. Implementation timelines run six to twelve months. Annual licensing costs are measured in hundreds of thousands of dollars. The validation burden requires dedicated IT and quality resources that a ten-person biotech does not have. And critically, they solve the case processing and submission problem — they do not solve the intake problem. You still need to get structured data into the system, and most enterprise platforms assume that data arrives from a clinical database or a CRO, not from an unstructured phone call with a site coordinator.

Small biotech programs need something different. They need a structured intake layer that captures the right data at first contact, manages the case through a compliant workflow, and produces E2B(R3) ready output — without requiring a six-month implementation or a dedicated PV IT team to maintain it.

Who It Is Built For

AE Connect is designed for three types of organizations:

Early-phase biotech companies with active INDs that are handling adverse event intake through ad hoc processes and need a validated, compliant system of record before their next FDA inspection or audit. These organizations often have one or two people handling all drug safety functions and cannot afford to spend six months implementing an enterprise platform.

Clinical-stage biotechs transitioning from CRO-managed pharmacovigilance to an in-house function. As programs mature, sponsors frequently bring PV in-house to reduce cost and improve oversight — and need a platform that can support an internal team without the infrastructure overhead of enterprise software.

Small pharma organizations with established safety programs that need a dedicated intake layer to interface with an existing safety database. AE Connect integrates with any safety database, including the one an organization already runs. That database remains the system of record.

The Bottom Line

The pharmacovigilance software market has had an unaddressed gap for years. Enterprise tools are too large, too expensive, and too operationally complex for early-phase biotech programs. Nothing purpose-built for small sponsors existed between those platforms and a shared spreadsheet. That gap is where compliance risk lives — in the unstructured intake process, the missing audit trail, the reconstructed case narrative, and the missed 7-day clock.

AE Connect was built by pharmacovigilance practitioners who have worked in that gap firsthand. It is not a scaled-down enterprise platform. It is a purpose-built intake and case management system designed for the actual resource constraints and compliance obligations of a small biotech drug safety program.
