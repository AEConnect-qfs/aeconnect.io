Special situation reports in pharmacovigilance: an intake guide

QORO Team · 18 November 2026 · about 10 minutes

A special situation does not look like an adverse event, and it is still a case to keep. A double dose with no symptoms, exposure before conception, a wrong strength caught in the pharmacy, and a vaccine that did not work are easy to close in email, because the note says there was no reaction. Closing them drops the outcome, the near miss, and the lack of effect. That puts patient safety at risk. The drug safety inbox is where these messages get lost. This page is what PV case intake should capture, when the record becomes an ICSR, and what follow up is still due. It is not a standard operating procedure. The reporter forms page names these observations in brief.

Other observations, and the four minimum criteria

ICH E2D(R1), adopted on 15 September 2025, section 2.1.5 lists other observations: exposure in pregnancy or breastfeeding, lack of efficacy or lack of effect, overdose, abuse, misuse, medication error, occupational exposure, and off label use. Each can arrive with an adverse event or with none. Section 2.2 says an ICSR needs one adverse event or other observation, one suspect or interacting product, an identifiable patient, and an identifiable reporter. Missing a criterion, collect it before you submit. Section 5.1.3 says to record the observation and, as applicable, follow it up, and to submit an ICSR with no adverse event only if the region requires it. Discuss that record in the periodic report under the ICH E2C guidelines where those apply. The ICSR intake guide is the field list once a case exists.

The US definition is not the ICH list

21 CFR 314.80(a) includes, in an adverse drug experience, an event from overdose whether accidental or intentional, from drug abuse, from drug withdrawal, and any failure of expected pharmacological action. 21 CFR 600.80(a) uses the same list for a biological product. A double dose with no event is an other observation. It is not, on that wording, an adverse event occurring from overdose. "It did not work" is the failure limb, so it sits inside the US definition. The 15 calendar day alert still requires serious and unexpected, from initial receipt. A lack of effect that is not serious can sit in the US periodic report. See aggregate reports and the AE and SAE page. An event after the product is stopped is the withdrawal limb. A defect in the same email is also a product quality complaint.

Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and paternal exposure

Section 5.1.3.3 covers exposure through a parent, including paternal exposure and breastfeeding. With no adverse event in the parent or the child, it is an ICSR only if the region requires it. The company is not expected to record uneventful exposure for a product indicated in pregnancy. Abnormal outcomes are reported: congenital anomalies, potential epigenetic responses, developmental disorders, fetal death, spontaneous abortion, and events in the mother or the newborn.

EU GVP Module VI (Rev 2) section VI.B.6.1 follows exposure through the mother or the father for the outcome and the child's development. A long half life means use before conception can still count. Do not submit an exposure with no outcome, a normal outcome, or an induced termination with no malformation detail. Collect those for the PSUR. Submit a no reaction exposure when the authorisation or the risk management plan requires it, including thalidomide or isotretinoin. ICH E2D(R1) section 6.4.1.2 expects follow up of all pregnancy reports to collect the outcome, newborn health, and child development where appropriate, unless the product is indicated in pregnancy and there is no event. GVP recommends a structured questionnaire. Outcome news received later is follow up on the same case, and the follow up guide covers that clock. If the parent and the child or foetus both have suspected reactions (other than early spontaneous abortion or foetal demise), GVP Module VI section VI.C.6.2.3.1 asks for two linked reports, one for the parent and one for the child, so a separate child case is not automatically a duplicate.

Overdose, abuse, misuse, medication error, occupational exposure

GVP section VI.A.1.2 keeps the terms apart. Overdose is a quantity above the maximum recommended dose in the authorised product information. Off label use is intentional use for a medical purpose outside the marketing authorisation. Misuse is intentional and inappropriate use outside those terms. Abuse is persistent or sporadic, intentional excessive use, with harmful physical or psychological effects. Occupational exposure comes from occupation, not from an ingredient before the finished product is released. A medication error is an unintended failure in the drug treatment process that leads to, or has the potential to lead to harm to the patient.

With no event, section 5.1.3.2 says submit only if the region requires it. A near miss that never reached a patient may still be required, and then the minimum criteria apply except an identifiable patient. In the EU, section VI.B.6.3 says not to submit when there is no suspected reaction. Record it for the PSUR, and follow up a report that does have a reaction. GVP points to the medication error guide EMA/762563/2014. Off label use with no event, in section 5.1.3.4, is the same regional test.

Lack of efficacy

With no event, lack of efficacy is an ICSR only if the region requires it. The US definition already includes any failure of expected pharmacological action, and the 15 day alert still needs serious and unexpected. In the EU, collect the report for the PSUR and do not submit it without a suspected reaction, unless the product is for a critical condition, a life threatening disease, a vaccine, or a contraceptive, and the reporter has not blamed disease progression. That 15 day path does not apply to a post authorisation efficacy study that is not interventional. Submit lack of prophylactic effect for a vaccine. Do not submit an antibiotic that was the wrong match for the organism.

Age

GVP section VI.B.6.2: make a reasonable attempt to obtain age or age group, including when there is no event. Age can also identify the patient.

What to capture before the thread is closed

Every row also needs the reporter, a way to reach them, the product in their words, the receipt date, and whether an event is present.

Two start dates

Section 5.2: an expedited ICSR goes no later than 15 calendar days after day zero, the date any company personnel, including a contractor, first has the minimum criteria, unless the region sets another date. The US alert uses initial receipt for a serious unexpected experience. Keep both. A pregnancy note with no problems can have a receipt date and no submission day zero, and it still needs a follow up date.

FAQ

Is a pregnancy exposure an ICSR if nobody reports an adverse event?

Only if the region requires it. A product indicated in pregnancy, with no event, need not be recorded. In the EU, no outcome and a normal outcome go to the PSUR unless the authorisation, the risk management plan, or a product such as thalidomide or isotretinoin requires a submission. Abnormal outcomes are reported.

What should intake capture when a medication error never reached a patient?

Where the process failed, and that no dose reached a patient. A near miss is an ICSR only where the region requires it, and then the patient criterion is waived. In the EU it is not submitted without a suspected reaction, but it is recorded for the PSUR because the definition includes potential harm.

Is lack of efficacy an adverse drug experience under FDA rules?

Yes. 21 CFR 314.80(a) and 21 CFR 600.80(a) include any failure of expected pharmacological action. The 15 calendar day alert still requires serious and unexpected. In the EU it is normally not an ICSR without a suspected reaction, with a 15 day path for critical conditions, life threatening diseases, vaccines, and contraceptives, unless the reporter states disease progression.

What follow up does a pregnancy report need?

The outcome, newborn health, and child development where appropriate, after exposure through the mother or the father. Not when the product is indicated in pregnancy and there is no event. Keep the later news on the same case. If the child also has a suspected reaction, GVP asks for a separate child report linked to the parent report.
