PV case intake forms: what a small team should capture

QORO Team · 23 October 2026 · about 6 minutes

Pharmacovigilance case intake is the hour when a report stops being an email and becomes a case. A PV case intake form is how a small drug-safety team captures that hour in one place. The form is not a safety database, and filling it in is not adverse event reporting to FDA or EMA. It is the record a qualified person needs before anyone can decide seriousness, expectedness, or whether a clock is running.

What intake is for

Sites, CROs, call centers, and medical monitors do not speak in E2B(R3) fields. They send a PDF, a three-line email, or a product quality complaint that happens to mention a patient. Intake is the translation step. Someone records the source wording, the dates, and the product, then a reviewer decides what the case is. Teams that skip this step retype the story later, usually after the weekend, and then argue about Day 0.

The drug safety inbox is where that delay actually happens. The intake form is the way out of the inbox. Full pharmacovigilance — submissions, signals, aggregate reports, a system of record — starts after intake. The difference is spelled out in AE intake versus full pharmacovigilance.

The four elements that make a case valid

ICH practice, used for both clinical and post-approval cases, treats an individual case as valid when four elements are present:

  • Identifiable reporter. A person who can be contacted. A name and a role are enough to start. An anonymous tweet with no way back is not.
  • Identifiable patient. Initials, a subject number, age and sex, or a chart reference. The patient does not have to be named in the intake file you store.
  • Suspect product. The drug, biologic, or device under study or on the market. Dose and lot help. A missing lot does not erase an adverse event.
  • Adverse event. What happened, in the reporter's words, with onset if you have it. A coding term can wait. The verbatim cannot.

If one element is missing, the case is a follow-up, not a discard. The intake form should show which element is missing and who was asked for it. That single habit prevents a serious report from sitting in a mailbox because the subject ID was in the attachment.

What else a useful intake form holds

Minimum criteria open the case. The next fields are what a reviewer needs for AE and SAE reporting and, when the criteria are met, SUSAR reporting.

  • Dates: when the sponsor or designee first received the report, when the event started, and when the product was given. Receipt date is the one teams lose.
  • Seriousness criteria, left blank until a qualified person marks them. Do not let a checkbox on a site form become the sponsor's decision without a look.
  • Outcome, if known, and whether the event was still going on at the time of the report.
  • The reference safety document used for expectedness: Investigator's Brochure or company core data sheet, with a version. The form can name the document. It should not pretend to be the document.
  • Concomitant products and the medical history that would change causality.
  • For a quality defect, lot or batch, and whether a patient was involved. That split is the subject of product quality complaint reporting.

Intake forms versus the safety database

Oracle Argus, ArisGlobal LifeSphere, and Veeva Vault Safety all have their own case-entry screens. Those screens are the right system of record once a company is running one of them. They are a poor place to discover, on a Sunday, that a coordinator's email was a serious report. A separate intake step is still useful beside those systems: read the source, draft the assessment, then have a person enter or reconcile the official case. FAQ

What is a PV case intake form?

A PV case intake form is the first structured record of a possible adverse event: who reported it, which patient it concerns, which product is suspected, and what happened. It is the front door of pharmacovigilance case processing. It is not the regulatory submission.

What is the minimum information for a valid individual case?

Four elements are widely treated as the minimum for a valid individual case safety report: an identifiable reporter, an identifiable patient, a suspect product, and an adverse event or reaction. A case can be opened for follow-up when one element is still thin. Your SOP should say who decides the case is valid and when the clock starts.

Does AE Connect replace the intake screens in Argus, LifeSphere, or Veeva?

No. If a safety database is the system of record, its screens stay the official entry. AE Connect turns an email, PDF, or pasted narrative into a reviewed draft. It does not write cases into Oracle Argus, ArisGlobal LifeSphere, or Veeva Vault Safety.
