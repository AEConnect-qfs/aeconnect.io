SUSAR triage before the case is booked

A SUSAR is a suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction. Three tests have to be true: serious, unexpected against the reference safety information, and suspected of being related. AE Connect drafts the inputs to those tests. It does not file the SUSAR.

What triage looks like here

The draft flags seriousness and expectedness and proposes a WHO-UMC causality term. A reviewer accepts or overrides each one. The timeline in the draft is a prompt for the 7-day and 15-day clocks a qualified person already knows from 21 CFR 312.32 and ICH E2A. The software does not start the regulatory clock and it does not send the report.

Where the SUSAR is filed

Filing stays with the safety database or the CRO named in the pharmacovigilance plan. If that system is Oracle Argus, ArisGlobal LifeSphere, or Veeva Vault Safety, AE Connect works in front of it. The reviewed draft is what the processor uses when they book the case. Read the SUSAR reporting guide for the clocks, and aggregate reports for why one SUSAR is not a DSUR.

Questions

Does AE Connect submit a SUSAR?

No. It drafts seriousness, expectedness, and causality for a person to review.

Does a SUSAR replace the DSUR?

No. Expedited cases and the annual development safety update are different documents. The annual report stays outside AE Connect.

Which safety database does the filing?

The one your plan names. AE Connect does not pick it and does not log into it.
